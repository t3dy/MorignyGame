# MORIGNY — Bibliography & Research Queue

The scholarship this game is answerable to. The reading program's job is to
move claims from the Research Queue into the data files with citations.

## Primary orbit (read first, read carefully)

- **Claire Fanger, *Rewriting Magic: An Exegesis of the Visionary
  Autobiography of a Fourteenth-Century French Monk*** (Penn State UP,
  2015). The canonical interpretive source *and* the model for the pencil
  hand: the scholar written into the narrative. Extract: John's biography
  and chronology; the sexual-temptation and scrupulosity material and how
  Fanger handles its register; her account of the Old/New Compilation
  rewriting; her reflexive method.
- **Claire Fanger & Nicholas Watson (eds.), *John of Morigny, Liber florum
  celestis doctrine / The Flowers of Heavenly Teaching*** (PIMS). The
  critical edition. Extract: the actual structure of the work (books,
  procedures, prayer sequences, hours), the Book of Visions episode
  catalog, the figures, Bridget material, textual variants for the witness/
  stemma system.
- **Nicholas Watson, "John the Monk's *Book of Visions*…"** in Fanger (ed.),
  *Conjuring Spirits: Texts and Traditions of Medieval Ritual Magic* (Penn
  State UP, 1998) — the essay that announced the modern rediscovery
  (McMaster manuscript); source for the framing ending.
- **Fanger & Watson, "The Prologue to John of Morigny's *Liber Visionum*:
  Text and Translation,"** *Esoterica* 3 (2001) — freely available online;
  first drafting reference for John's voice until the edition is on the desk.

## Context shelf

- **Richard Kieckhefer, *Magic in the Middle Ages*** (CUP) — the "clerical
  underworld" frame; and ***Forbidden Rites*** (the Munich necromantic
  handbook; epilogue/apparatus layer only, it is fifteenth-century).
- **Julien Véronèse**, critical editions and studies of the ***ars
  notoria*** — what John actually practiced, its prayers and *notae*.
- **Sophie Page, *Magic in the Cloister*** (Penn State UP, 2013) — monks
  collecting and domesticating magic texts; the institutional texture of
  monastic magic.
- **Frank Klaassen, *The Transformations of Magic*** (Penn State UP, 2013) —
  the long arc by which ritual magic was purified/"whitened" toward
  Renaissance learned magic; spine of the epilogue layer.
- **Claire Fanger (ed.), *Invoking Angels: Theurgic Ideas and Practices,
  Thirteenth to Sixteenth Centuries*** (Penn State UP, 2012) — includes her
  further work on John.
- **Benedek Láng, *Unlocked Books*** — the Central European manuscript
  afterlife; witness/stemma flavor.
- **Barbara Newman, "What Did It Mean to Say 'I Saw'?"** (*Speculum*, 2005)
  — how medieval visionary experience was cultivated and reported; design
  spine for the vision/discernment system.
- **Mary Carruthers, *The Book of Memory*** — memory arts; the ars notoria's
  promise of infused knowledge sits inside this world.
- **Michael Camille, *Image on the Edge*** — the Gothic margin; warrant and
  grammar for the drollery-pressure mechanic.
- ***RB 1980: The Rule of St. Benedict*** (ed. Fry) — the Rule in Latin and
  English with commentary; source for the horarium and dormitory staging.
