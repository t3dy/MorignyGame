## 0. The sentence that reframes the whole project

> "The reader is supposed to follow John's path rather than merely
> absorb John's conclusions." — on why the *Book of Visions* is itself
> a pedagogical artifact

Fanger's argument is that John's autobiography is not a memoir attached
to a ritual manual. It is a **teaching device whose method is
re-enactment**: the *Liber florum* "tries to reproduce the process by
which John himself came to know." A book that works by making the
reader walk the author's path, error by error, is a book whose natural
modern form is a game. We have been building the right object without
being able to say why. Now we can say why, and it should be visible in
the apparatus.

---

## 1. THE CORE VERB: he adjures, he does not conjure

The single most useful distinction in either document.

| | Necromancer (Kieckhefer) | Notory operator (John) |
|---|---|---|
| Speech act | **conjures** — commands, constrains, bargains | **adjures** — petitions, appeals, invokes |
| Where efficacy lives | in the magician's knowledge and correct performance | in the divinely established order of signs |
| Posture | mastery | **humility** — "begs for divine contact in all humility" |
| Who initiates contact | the operator summons | the operator *disposes himself and waits* |
| How demons arrive | **summoned** | **they intrude** |

That last row is the game's whole horror, and we have not built it.
Kieckhefer's magician crosses a boundary deliberately and knows it.
John is trying to cross in the *opposite* direction — toward God — and
the supernatural answers, and **he cannot reliably tell who is
answering**. The ars notoria becomes, in the digest's phrase, "a machine
for producing epistemological uncertainty."

**Design consequence — and this is the shape of the whole game (decided
2026-08-31): adjuration and conjuration are the two MODES OF RITUAL
AGENCY, and choosing between them is the axis the campaign turns on.**
See §11. In brief: the historical John adjures, and the game must let
you play that superbly; but the player may also take him toward the
"dirty magician" — command, constraint, performance — with the record's
own evidence carrying him a surprisingly long way before anything
becomes counterfactual.

Within the adjuring mode specifically:
- No mechanic lets John compel an outcome. He prepares, petitions, waits.
- Preparation raises the chance of an answer and never determines its
  content — including the answers *no* and *nothing*.
- Demonic intrusion happens **during legitimate practice**, not as
  punishment for illegitimate practice. Historically exact, and the
  more frightening version.

## 2. THE ART IS A DEVOTIONAL PEDAGOGY, NOT A SPELLBOOK

John does not use the ars notoria to get *facts*. He uses it as "a
technology for making a person capable of learning" — and the proof is
Bridget.

**Bridget, precisely as documented:** fifteen years old; she repeatedly
asks him to teach her to read; he thinks fifteen is *too old* to become
a good reader; he decides to put her through the art's learning process
anyway; he teaches her the letters; within six months she reads **and
writes**; he boasts that she can sing an Alleluia in church unassisted.
Then the demon comes.

This was not bizarre in context: girls commonly learned literacy through
prayer books, missals, antiphonals and Hours of the Virgin, so the ars
notoria's repetitive Latin and mnemonic structure looked like an
unusually powerful version of a familiar pedagogy. **The magic migrates
from the university into the household.**

**Against Hugh of St Victor** (the *Didascalicon*, which John inherits
and radicalises):

- Hugh: moral discipline → properly formed student → better learning.
- John: ritual/devotional discipline → **transformed knower** → access
  to knowledge previously inaccessible.

Fanger's formulation to keep on the wall: *"devotion was engaged with
the aim of learning, and learning was engaged with the aim of
devotion."* Reciprocal, not sequential.

**Design consequences — this rewrites the faculties system:**
- Faculties are currently accumulated study-hours, which is Hugh's
  ladder. John's model is that **capacity depends on disposition**:
  learning is an effect of grace whose efficacy depends on the state of
  the recipient. A faculty should be *available* only to the extent
  John is properly disposed — purity, confession current, observance
  kept — not merely *earned* once and banked.
- Teaching someone should be a first-class action, not flavour. It is
  what John actually did, it is how the practice spread, and it is what
  made the 1323 condemnation about a *movement* rather than a monk.
- Hugh's good student is "humble, docile, diligent, willing to learn
  from anyone, suspicious of pride, concerned with **becoming** learned
  rather than **appearing** learned." That is a virtue rubric the game
  can price directly.

## 3. KNOWING vs DOING: the artes exceptivae

John's prayer for necromancy asks, in effect: *"May I know and
understand necromancy, but not perform it."* He builds a taxonomy —
aeromancy, pyromancy, chiromancy, geomancy, geonegia, hydromancy — and
gives the forbidden arts **a place in the architecture of knowledge**
while excluding their practice.

Hugh could simply exclude magic from real knowledge. John, a century
later, cannot: the influx of Arabic, Jewish, and astrological writing
has destabilised the boundaries of "knowledge" itself. So he does
something stranger — magic is **excluded as a practice but retained as
knowledge**.

**Design consequence (decided):** a knowledge tree in which the
forbidden arts can be *learned* legitimately, even piously — and in
which **performing them is a real, playable option**, separate and
marked. The gap between knowing and doing is where the whole drama of
temptation lives, which is exactly where Fanger puts it; making
performance unavailable would collapse that gap and hand the player a
sermon. Performance is the doorway into the second mode (§11).

## 4. THE RECURSIVE LOOP (the day, and the campaign)

Hugh's model is a ladder. John's is a spiral, and the spiral is the game
loop:

```
learn → vision → interpret → WRITE A PRAYER → perform the prayer
      → new vision → discover the previous practice was defective
      → REWRITE THE SYSTEM → perform the new system → another revelation
```

"Knowledge changes the practice that produces knowledge."

Two mechanics fall directly out of this:

**(a) Composition.** A vision becomes a written prayer, which enters
John's own book, which changes later practice, which produces the next
vision. We have copying; we have no composition, and composition is the
engine.

**(b) The inception dream / worthiness gate.** The ars notoria's own
preliminary prayers produce a dream in which an angel tells the operator
**whether he is worthy to proceed** — and later, when the operator errs
or stalls, a further dream instructs him whether and how to continue.
This is a gorgeous, fully attested structure and it is the honest form
of a tutorial: the game asks permission of its own fiction.

## 5. THEURGY — a historian's category, used carefully

Fanger's use of "theurgy" is **not** a claim that John thought of
himself as a theurgist. It is an analytical tool, and she is explicit
that it should not replace medieval categories. Three levels to hold
apart:

1. what practitioners called what they did,
2. what theologians thought they were doing,
3. what modern historians call it to analyse its structure.

Crucially, Fanger differs from Véronèse here: he uses "theurgy" partly
to *separate* the ars notoria from the demonological complex; **she uses
it because it engages those problems rather than escaping them.**
Theurgy is not a synonym for "good magic." It **names the contested
middle**:

```
liturgical prayer → sacramental ritual → theurgical practice →
angelic invocation → ambiguous magic → demonic magic / nigromancy
```

— with enormous overlap and boundaries that *medieval authors were
themselves fighting over*. The ars notoria calls its own prayers
"sacramental mysteries"; Peter of Abano defends the comparison; Aquinas
rejects it, because the art was **not divinely instituted**.

Which surfaces the question underneath everything:

> **Who authorized the ritual?**

That is why John needs the Virgin. His entire solution is to establish a
legitimate divine authorization for his replacement art. Our `licentia`
mechanic is therefore not a resource — it is *the answer to the central
theological question of his life*, and we have been treating it as gold
paint.

**And the sacred-language theory arrives with it:** the mysterious words
work *without the operator understanding them* — efficacy does not
depend on semantic comprehension. Our `verba ignota` mechanic (errors in
the unknown words admit no correction-from-sense) is not a clever
invention; it is this theory, and the apparatus should say so.

## 6. THE RETARGETED STRUGGLE (decided; see DECISIONS)

Confirmed by both digests: **the desire for forbidden knowledge is the
temptation.** The devil notices John when he encounters a necromantic
book, and the temptation *succeeds* — he begins working out how to
acquire expertise. And Fanger's striking turn: John asks for
comprehensive knowledge **so that he may restrain himself from the
impious way**. Knowledge is simultaneously the temptation and the
requested defence against temptation.

The loop, in Fanger's terms:

```
sin → temptation → desire for knowledge → magical experiment →
demonic deception → recognition → confession → purification →
divinely authorized knowledge → ability to resist further temptation
```

That is a better engine than the one it replaces, and it is *load
bearing for everything else in this document*.

## 7. JOHN AS HE ACTUALLY WAS

Corrections to our portrait:

- Benedictine monk **and priest**; **canon law degree from Orléans**;
  **provost of Morigny after 1308**, administering rents and tithes.
  Institutional trust, not anonymity.
- He obtained the ars notoria **through the university library**; a
  **physician from Bologna (Jacob)** presented it to him as the *holy
  alternative* to nigromancia. He came to it as the safe option.
- He got a necromancy book from a cleric **first**, and copied as much
  of it as he could.
- Mocked as **Sompniator** — "dreamer," with the implication of
  magician.
- His critics: the **"Barking Dogs,"** who attacked the figures for
  their crosses, circles, and planetary cycles.
- Real places and people to use: **Autruy** (his mother's house, and a
  vision set beside it), **Chartres** (the vision at about thirteen —
  the devil, and Mary's rescue: the foundational scene of his life),
  **John of Fontainejean** (Cistercian friend and second student).

## 8. THE BOOK THAT REMEMBERS ITS OWN ERRORS

The New Compilation keeps John's mistaken instructions **visible**, with
corrective glosses added so later operators will not follow them; the
flowers and leaves around those passages are, in his own account, a sign
of the chastisement of his error. "His errors are not erased; they are
preserved as part of the sacred history of the book."

And the manuscripts were **used**: many personalised, with operators
writing their own names into the prayers and figures — one witness
(Klagenfurt, Cart. 1) is a **template with blank spaces left for the
operator's name**.

This is, almost exactly, the editable playthrough-log we planned in
`docs/V4_LOOP_REDESIGN.md` §7 — except historical. The log should
preserve emendations as visible corrections rather than replacing text,
and a published witness should carry the reader's own name into the
blanks.

## 9. THE MILK AND THE MEAT

The Old Compilation is spiritual childhood: John sleeps at Mary's
breast, plays with the infant Christ, and **freely invents figures**.
The New is maturity: Mary gives solid food, and he must stretch out his
arms and be girded and led where he does not choose to go. His error was
**acting before the promised instruction came** — a failure of obedience
and discernment, not of doctrine.

A two-act campaign shape is sitting right there: an early game of
invention and licence, and a late game of submission and correction, with
the Barking Dogs' attack as the hinge.

## 11. THE TWO MODES — the axis the campaign turns on (decided)

*Ted, 2026-08-31: showcase what Fanger reveals, **and** let the player
take John toward the "dirty magician" (the phrase borrowed from Frances
Yates `[verify]`) or the heretic — with real variation in encounter
outcomes, careers, and social and political trajectories, driven by
choices, skill investment, and how the player engages the world.*

Adjuration and conjuration stop being a historical footnote and become
the game's two modes of ritual agency. Every ritual act declares which
one it is.

| | **Adjuration** — the record's John | **Conjuration** — the dirty magician |
|---|---|---|
| Speech act | petitions, appeals, invokes | commands, constrains, bargains |
| Efficacy | in the divine order; *he cannot compel* | **in the operator** — it works because he is good at it |
| Preparation | purity, confession, fasting, observance | apparatus, timing, names, correct performance |
| Reliability | uncertain; God may say no, or nothing | **higher, and that is the temptation** |
| Failure | ambiguity, silence, intrusion | catastrophe, and it is legible |
| Cost | fatigue, resolve, scruple, waiting | suspicion, heresy risk, and the soul |
| Who is teaching | unknown — the epistemological horror | known, and that is its own horror |

**The design rule that keeps both honest:** adjuration must not be the
"good but weak" option. It is *slower and less certain*, and it is the
only road on which the licentia (§5) can ever be granted — which is the
only thing that makes a copy authorized, a witness blessed, and a
transmission worth having. Conjuration works *now*. Adjuration is the
only thing that works *after 1323*.

### The three-stage runway (this is the important structural point)

The record does not put John on one side of this line. Playing the
transgressive road is not immediately counterfactual, and the game
should be precise about where the line actually is:

1. **Attested transgression — not counterfactual at all.** John got a
   necromancy book from a cleric and copied as much as he could. He
   sought out Jacob of Bologna. He worked the **Four Rings of Solomon**.
   He kept necromantic books after his conversion and worried about
   what his community would think. He was composing a **nova
   nigromancia** of his own. A player can do all of this and still be
   inside the record. Nothing is annotated; the pencil hand simply
   cites where each act comes from.
2. **Extrapolation — marked, but supportable.** Continuing the
   performance, taking commissions, building a reputation, refusing the
   moment of recognition that historically came. The pencil hand notes
   that the record turns here and this witness did not.
3. **Departure — the annotation fires.** *"Here the witness departs
   from the record."* (`CLAUDE.md` rule 5, unchanged and binding.) The
   run is a contaminated witness in the stemma, and the Bruno/dirty-
   magician endings become reachable.

That runway is a gift: the player gets a long, genuinely historical
stretch of transgression before the game has to admit it is inventing —
and the moment it does admit it lands much harder for the delay.

### Where the variation comes from

Ted's ask is breadth of outcome, and these are the axes the encounter
pool and career system should read (`docs/ENCOUNTER_SYSTEMS_STUDY.md`
mechanisms already support all of it):

- **mode** — adjuring / conjuring, per act and as an accumulated habit
- **faculties** — and, per §2, how much of them disposition makes
  available
- **career** — provost, obedientiary office, ecclesiastical advancement
  vs. underworld standing; these should be *rival* ladders, not one bar
- **social ties** — Bridget, John of Fontainejean, patrons, the Barking
  Dogs; who trusts him, who has cause to depose about him
- **the risk bag** — heresy, exposure, debt, scandal; already built
- **what got out** — the stemma, which is still the score

## 10. What this costs us

Honest accounting of what these directions *break*:

- The Struggle content (night deliberations, tier text, verbs, outcomes)
  is retargeted — a substantial rewrite of shipped writing.
- `CLAUDE.md` rule 3 and the launch content note must change.
- The faculties system as built (banked study hours) is Hugh's model,
  not John's, and wants regrounding on disposition.
- The daylight block gains composition and provost duties; the
  scriptorium becomes one option among several rather than the centre.
- Several `sources` loci across the content overclaim Fanger and need
  correcting, not just extending.

None of that is regrettable. All of it is the scholarship being
stranger and better than what we invented, which `CLAUDE.md` rule 9
predicted it would be.
