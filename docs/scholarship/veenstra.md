## Their key intervention
Veenstra's *Magic and Divination at the Courts of Burgundy and France* is a critical edition and contextual study of Laurens Pignon's 1411 treatise *Contre les devineurs* ("Against the Diviners"), written for the Burgundian court. Veenstra reconstructs the social and intellectual world in which magicians, astrologers, and diviners actually operated at a great late-medieval court — one generation-plus after John, but in the same broader French-speaking ecclesiastical-political world — and shows how a theologically trained cleric (Pignon) used Thomistic argument to attack court divination as both spiritually dangerous and intellectually incoherent. The book's larger value is methodological: it treats a magic-accusation text not as a transparent record of what magicians did, but as a document of how theological argument was mobilized *against* magic in a specific political context, with specific patrons and specific stakes — a caution against reading any inquisitorial or condemnatory source (including the *Grandes Chroniques*' 1323 notice about John) as neutral reportage.
