# Fabrizio Conti

## Who they are
Historian at John Cabot University in Rome (listed variously as Lecturer/Adjunct Professor/Associate Professor of History across different CV versions [exact current rank verify]), also affiliated with Università Roma Tre. Research spans the history of magic and witchcraft across late antique, medieval, and Renaissance periods.

## Core works relevant to this game
- ***Witchcraft, Superstition, and Observant Franciscan Preachers: Pastoral Approach and Intellectual Debate in Renaissance Milan*** (Brepols, 2015).
- Editor, *Civilizations of the Supernatural: Witchcraft, Ritual, and Religious Experience in Late Antique, Medieval, and Renaissance Traditions* (Trivent Publishing, 2020).
- A monograph on magic in the Roman world, published 2022 per John Cabot University's own announcement [exact title verify].

## Their key intervention
Conti's work on Observant Franciscan preachers (Bernardino of Siena, John of Capistrano, Giacomo della Marca, and others) examines how reform-minded fifteenth-century preachers built their pastoral and homiletic practice specifically around identifying, denouncing, and re-educating the laity out of "superstitious" practices — while simultaneously engaging in genuine, often sophisticated intellectual debate among themselves about where exactly the line between superstition and licit devotion fell. His work shows that the boundary-drawing Bailey identifies at the scholastic/theological level (see `bailey.md`) had a direct pastoral, on-the-ground counterpart: popular preaching was itself a site where the magic/religion boundary was actively taught, contested, and enforced at the level of ordinary lay practice, not just debated in university disputations.

## Narrative & mechanical implications for MORIGNY
- Conti's pastoral/preaching angle is a useful, currently-missing register for this game's community-suspicion mechanics (`DESIGN.md`'s Suspicion & Secrecy, the horarium's chapter-of-faults beat): the community around John — his abbot, his confessor, his brothers — would plausibly have absorbed exactly this kind of preached boundary-vocabulary about superstition, giving concrete period-texture to *why* a brother glimpsing a figure or noticing candle use at odd hours would react with suspicion rather than curiosity.
- His focus is Observant Franciscan and specifically fifteenth-century (a century-plus after John, in Renaissance Milan) — squarely later-period material, like Kieckhefer's *Forbidden Rites* and Klaassen's core evidence base. His relevance to this project's core 1280–1330 play window is therefore comparative/structural only; any direct use of his specific findings belongs in the whitening-epilogue register (`DESIGN.md`'s Whitening Arc), clearly dated on screen, not in core play.
- His edited volume's explicit framing — "civilizations of the supernatural" spanning late antique through Renaissance — models the kind of long-durée, cross-period thinking this game's own whitening epilogue already attempts (John's fourteenth-century condemnation situated against a much longer arc running through Ficino, Trithemius, Agrippa); Conti's own scholarly generation (a working historian actively publishing as of 2020–2022) shows this field remains an active, ongoing scholarly conversation rather than a settled historiography — worth naming in the synthesis document as evidence the "reading program" (`BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`) is not chasing a closed body of work.
- Honest assessment: of the seventeen scholars in this set, Conti's direct bearing on anything currently built in MORIGNY is the thinnest — his period and geography (Renaissance Italy) are furthest from Morigny's early fourteenth-century northern France. This report should say so plainly rather than strain for relevance; his value is mainly as the most recent working exemplar of the broader field this project draws on, and as a comparative source for the whitening epilogue only.
