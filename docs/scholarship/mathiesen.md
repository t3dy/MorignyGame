# Robert Mathiesen

## Who they are
Professor Emeritus at Brown University. Originally trained in Slavic linguistics and medieval Slavic philology (joined Brown in 1967), his teaching and research shifted from the 1980s onward toward medieval manuscript studies and, from the 1990s, the history of magic and esotericism. Founding member of the Societas Magica, the first North American scholarly society devoted to the academic study of magic, occultism, and esoteric movements.

## Core works relevant to this game
- Work on the typology and manuscript tradition of the ***Clavicula Salomonis*** ("Key of Solomon") textual family — a major medieval/early modern Solomonic ritual-magic corpus related in spirit, though not identical, to the *ars notoria* tradition John practiced.
- Contributions to scholarship on ***Sepher ha-Razim*** ("The Book of Mysteries"), an early (Late Antique) Hebrew magical text with a documented afterlife influencing later medieval magical traditions [Mathiesen's precise role — translator, annotator, or commentator on specific editions — should be marked `[verify]`; the standard English translation is usually credited to Michael A. Morgan].
- Taught courses at Brown including "Magic in the Middle Ages," "Women, Magic and Power," and "Esoteric Russia" — indicating a research range spanning Western and Slavic/Orthodox magical traditions.

## Their key intervention
Mathiesen's distinctive contribution is manuscript-typological: rather than treating "the Key of Solomon" or similar grimoire titles as single stable texts, he works out how sprawling families of related-but-divergent manuscripts under a shared title actually relate to one another — which recensions share which content, which are later accretions, which represent genuinely distinct textual traditions merely sharing a name. This is exactly the kind of stemmatic, manuscript-descent thinking the *ars notoria* and *Liber florum* traditions also require (both survive in multiple divergent copies under related titles), and Mathiesen's broader career — bridging Western Solomonic magic and Slavic/Orthodox esoteric traditions — models the kind of cross-regional manuscript tracing this project's stemma system dramatizes in fictional miniature.

## Narrative & mechanical implications for MORIGNY
- Mathiesen's manuscript-typology method (sorting a sprawling, multi-titled textual family into actual lines of descent) is a direct real-world analogue of `src/engine/stemma.js`'s `buildStemma()` — assigning witnesses sigla, tracking inherited vs. own corruptions down a line of descent is exactly this kind of work, done here on an invented corpus instead of the real Solomonic manuscript tradition.
- His long institutional association with the Societas Magica (a body this game's own bibliography draws several other cited scholars from, including Kieckhefer as a past president) marks him as part of the same founding scholarly generation that established magic history as a legitimate academic field — useful context for the synthesis document's account of how this whole area of scholarship developed and professionalized, rather than a source of specific game content.
- If the design ever wants a Solomonic-tradition comparandum for John's own *notae*/figures beyond the *ars notoria* proper (e.g., how *Clavicula Salomonis* figures compare in their fidelity requirements), Mathiesen's typological work on that tradition would be the place to start — currently unbuilt, flagged as a possible future research thread rather than something the game needs now.
