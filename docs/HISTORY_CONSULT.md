# MORIGNY — Historian's Consult: Magic, Monasticism, and the Scriptorium

*2026-07-29. A working brief in the role of historian of medieval magic
and monasticism. Per `CLAUDE.md` rule 4 this is the designer-scholar's
own voice using the field's methods — it is not Claire Fanger, bears no
real scholar's name, and quotes no one without a citation that exists.
Claims below are graded: **solid** (standard scholarship, safe to build
on), **thin** (plausible, needs the Research Queue), **ours** (invention,
marked as such in data).*

---

## 1. What the scriptorium build stands on

### Solid — standard codicology and monastic history

- **The craft chain** (parchment preparation, quire structure and
  Gregory's Rule, pricking/ruling, iron-gall ink, pigments and their
  chemistry, gold before pigment, text-then-rubrication order, the
  penknife in the off hand): textbook material; SCRIPTORIUM.md §1
  summarizes it and §6 clears it. Safe at `status: attested` with the
  §1 summary as locus. When we want display-grade citations, De Hamel's
  *Scribes and Illuminators* and Clemens & Graham's *Introduction to
  Manuscript Studies* are the standard references to verify against —
  queue item R-9 below.
- **Named scribal errors** (homoeoteleuton/eyeskip, dittography,
  haplography; expunctuation; *signes-de-renvoi*): standard textual
  criticism; the engine's three classes are honest simplifications
  (haplography folded into eyeskip — disclose in the apparatus if a
  pedant asks; F-9).
- **RB 48 and the labor day; the armarius and sacrist as offices;
  silence in the scriptorium; the fire-and-candle prohibition as
  widespread custom**: solid as *types*. Our Brother Denis and Brother
  Maur are invented persons of attested offices — envelope `invented`
  with the office cited, same pattern as the Étampes townsfolk.
- **The colophon tradition** ("three fingers write, the whole body
  labors" and kin): a genuine medieval commonplace family, attested
  across many manuscripts. Cite as *commonplace*, never to a single
  invented manuscript. The pencil note does this correctly.
- **Pigment hazards**: orpiment is arsenic sulfide and does sicken;
  it does blacken in contact with lead- and copper-based pigments;
  verdigris is notoriously corrosive to parchment over time;
  ultramarine's cost is proverbial. All solid. Our *time compression*
  (verdigris in 8 game-days) is `sim` math and the materials file says
  so in its header — good practice, keep it.

### Solid — the magical-text specifics

- **The ars notoria runs on figures (notae) and verba ignota**, and an
  inaccurately copied figure was held to invalidate the operation:
  well-established in the Véronèse line of scholarship. The engine's
  best mechanic (unintelligible text has no error-correction) is a
  faithful rendering of a real transmission pathology.
- **Monks acquired, bound, and camouflaged magic texts** (Sophie Page's
  *Magic in the Cloister*, on St Augustine's Canterbury): the
  concealment table's "bound into a licit codex" row is documented
  survival strategy, not our invention.
- **John's Liber florum survived by copies leaving his custody**; the
  1323 burning is recorded in the *Grandes Chroniques* notice; the work
  was rediscovered via the McMaster manuscript (Watson 1998). The
  transmission thesis is the literal history.

### Thin — flagged `verify`, correctly

- **Pecia at Orléans.** The pecia system belongs to the universities
  (Paris, Bologna); Orléans was a law-school town and had a book trade,
  but pecia *there* is our extrapolation. Data says `adapted` +
  `verify` and cites the queue. Right call; do not promote without
  Page/Pollard reading (R-3).
- **John's own figure program** (how many figures, what they looked
  like, how the Old and New Compilations differ): everything specific
  is `verify` pending the Fanger–Watson edition (R-2). The engine's
  `figures: 1–2` per exemplar is placeholder arithmetic — the stage
  should not display figure *names* until the edition is on the desk.
- **The Old Compilation as a physical exemplar in John's custody at
  Morigny in the play period**: chronologically plausible,
  biographically unverified (R-2). Currently `adapted` + `verify` —
  correct.
- **Isabel's trade channel** (an Étampes stationer receiving Orléans
  student books): commercially plausible for the corridor, invented in
  the particular. Envelope is honest (`adapted` for the ars notoria
  fragment's *content*, invented persons around it).

### Ours — and honestly marked

- The five exemplars as *objects*, the two cloister brothers, all
  narration. All `invented`/`adapted` with register models cited.

## 2. Register cautions for the new writing

1. **Copying the Work is a devotional act performed by a penitent.**
   John's relation to his own earlier redaction is *rewriting as
   devotion* (the project's spine). The acquisition text for the Old
   Compilation must carry tenderness and dread in the same sentence,
   not the thrill of contraband. Save contraband affect for the pecia
   channel, where it is historically at home.
2. **The hazard events are not slapstick.** A poisoned scribe in 1315
   is a man whose body is failing him for reasons he half-understands.
   Orpiment sickness narration belongs to John's combat-and-weather
   idiom (STYLE_GUIDE, Struggle rules apply by analogy: felt, not
   depicted clinically, never comic).
3. **The armarius is not a warden and the sacrist is not a shopkeeper.**
   Both offices are devotional trusts held by brothers John lives with.
   Their menace (the ledger, the noticing) must be the menace of
   *community* — Kieckhefer's paperwork-fear (PACING §4), not a guard
   NPC. No keys jangling.
4. **Verba ignota on screen**: per SCRIPTORIUM.md §6, no invented
   strings presented as genuine. The stage never displays the words
   themselves — only their *shape* ("a line of the unknown words,
   letter by letter"). If we ever show strings, they are `invented`
   and the pencil says so on screen.

## 4. One structural opinion

The build keeps historical claims in envelopes and game arithmetic in
`sim` blocks, and the tests refuse records that blur them. From the
historian's chair this is the project's single most trustworthy habit:
it makes the inevitable inventions *auditable*. Keep the discipline
through the stage build — narration records for hazards and acquisition
should cite their register models exactly the way the night-outcome
records do, even though it is tempting to leave `sources: []` on
"mere" flavor. Flavor is where anachronism gets in.
